01 · The opportunity

The 8 findings

Finding 01 · Inbox debt

420 unanswered LinkedIn DMs sitting in Ben's inbox

Impact: At even a 5% qualified rate, that's 20+ warm prospects who already raised their hand and got ghosted. Some of those are silent disengagement from people who took the Marketing Leaders Research bait and got nothing back.

Fix (Week 1): Inbox triage. Surface qualified conversations. Book meetings from existing demand before sending a single new outbound message.

Finding 02 · Sequence craft

The Quiz campaign runs at 2x industry, but leaves meat on the bone

Observation: 800 leads, 376 accepted (48.6%), 128 replied (16.6%). Industry benchmark is 20 to 30% acceptance and 5 to 10% response.

Impact: Channel works and Ben's brand carries weight. Bottleneck is sequence craft, not list quality or platform fit.

Fix: Rewrite all sequences. Kill auto-actions. Lead with value observation, not "thank you for connecting." Add 4th to 6th touches with voice notes and document shares. Replace "would you like a copy?" CTAs with direct value drops.

Finding 03 · Wasted openers

The first message in every sequence is wasted

Observation: Campaign 1 opens with "Thank you for accepting my connection request." Campaign 2 opens with "How's your week going?"

Impact: The highest-attention real estate in the entire sequence (just-accepted, fresh notification) is burned on platitudes. Most prospects scroll past.

Fix: Replace with value-led openers. Specific observation about their work, content, role, or company. Or skip pleasantries entirely and go straight to the hook.

Finding 04 · Broken antecedent

The podcast pitch message has no antecedent

Observation: Campaign 2 message reads "It is a weekly podcast for business leaders that I run with Steph." But there's no prior reference for "It." Recipient reads cold and has to figure out what Ben's talking about.

Fix: Self-contained, specific framing. "Wanted to share something I think you'll find useful. I host a weekly podcast for B2B marketing leaders called Canned, with [Steph]. Recent episode on [topic relevant to ICP] is worth a listen if you're commuting this week."

Finding 05 · Brand-damaging automation

Skill endorsements and post-likes are publicly damaging Ben's brand

Observation: Both Dripify campaigns automatically endorse skills and like posts within hours of connecting. Campaign 2 runs Skill Endorsement twice in the same sequence.

Impact: These actions are public to the recipient's network. Ben is publicly endorsing strangers for skills he can't have assessed. Reads as bot behaviour to sophisticated buyers (which AU marketing leaders are). Undermines founder-level credibility.

Fix: Turn Skill Endorsement off entirely. Keep Like-a-Post as a measured signal. Day 1 fix, zero cost.

Finding 07 · Brand voice gap

HD's brand voice is completely absent from its own outbound

Observation: The HD Brand Guideline defines tone as Personable, Smart, Unexpected ("B2C flair to the B2B equation"). Brand pillars include "We do different," "There's nothing BAU about the way we B2B." The outbound reads like every other generic SDR campaign.

Impact: The most differentiated thing about HD (its voice) is invisible in the channel where differentiation matters most.

Fix: Rewrite sequences in HD's actual brand voice. Use the guideline as the style brief. "Where funnels meet fingerprints" is a copy resource, not just a tagline.

Finding 08 · No commercial bridge

No commercial conversion path in any lead magnet

Observation: Quiz → LinkedIn article. Calculator → 24-hour email. Research → university survey. Planner → "reply to request a copy."

Impact: Every asset captures intent but routes the prospect AWAY from commercial conversation. The path from "interested" to "in a meeting with Ben" is broken across every asset.

Fix: Every lead magnet must have a "book a 15-min teardown call" CTA on the result page. The result page IS the sales bridge. Currently it's a dead end.

03 · Where HumanDigital can win

Five wedges. One the incumbents can't see.

Mapped against the three named competitors. Each wedge is something HD can credibly own that they currently cannot.

WEDGE 01

Productised AI inside delivery

None of the three competitors has a visible AI-native service line. Green Hat talks about AI in research; Dentsu and Blue Ocean don't. HD has already signalled the move with Aether but hasn't productised it externally. "AI-accelerated growth ops" as a category is genuinely available.

WEDGE 02

Outbound and Dripify as a service

All three competitors rely on inbound, thought-leadership, and holding-group relationships. None visibly runs outbound social-selling or Dripify-style sequences for clients. HD sells "Signal-based outbound" but doesn't run it for itself. The engagement is the proof point.

WEDGE 03

Interactive lead-gen tools

Green Hat's "lead magnets" are 60-page PDF research reports. Blue Ocean has a podcast. Dentsu has an Index. None ships interactive lead-gen tools (scorecards, calculators, assessments). HD built one for ABB and doesn't have one for itself.

WEDGE 04

Senior-talent stability

Green Hat's Glassdoor pattern (60% turnover, comp dropping) and Dentsu's leadership exodus to Grove B2B both signal delivery risk for enterprise buyers. HD can lean into "the same operator from sale through delivery." Small-team strength reframed.

WEDGE 05

Trans-Tasman delivery

Blue Ocean is NZ-only with no Australian footprint. Green Hat and Dentsu are AU-centric with Singapore extension but no real NZ play. A NZ-founded agency that credibly operates both sides of the Tasman, using outbound + AI to do it efficiently, is a positioning lane that's effectively open. HD already has the AU office and recent AU hire to back the claim.

The strongest single wedge

"AI-powered outbound and lead magnets for B2B."

Combines Wedges 1, 2, and 3. Slots directly into the brief Ben outlined in WhatsApp. Sits in territory none of the three named competitors occupy. Maps to the exact services HD already lists but doesn't execute on for itself.

The 8-week engagement becomes "build HumanDigital's own proof of the services HumanDigital sells."
